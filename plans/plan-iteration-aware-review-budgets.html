TARS: Iteration-Aware Review Budgets

Issue #10 — max_rounds currently caps the global handoff round value. Plan revisions, planned implementation iterations, approvals, and reviewer-requested corrections all consume that one sequence, so a valid multi-iteration lane can block even when it has not exceeded its intended review-retry budget. The fix treats round as ordering/correlation metadata only and introduces a reviewer-selected, separately persisted review budget for the implementation phase.

Problem details

  • ReviewLoopCoordinator.dispatchLane (automations/review-loop/lib/coordinator.mjs, line ~105) blocks the lane whenever event.round > lane.maxRounds (default 5). Only author-authored handoffs advance round — the plan-review request and each implementation-response — while reviewer verdicts and code reviews preserve the round of the handoff they respond to. Because the cap is enforced against the whole workflow, it cannot tell a requested retry from ordinary iteration structure.
  • A plan-first lane that needs two planning revisions and then runs all three approved iterations publishes author handoffs at rounds 2 through 6 (initial plan-review at 1; revised plan-review requests at 2 and 3; the three implementation responses at 4, 5, and 6). With no implementation-phase changes_requested, round reaches 6 and the default cap of 5 blocks the lane as blocked (reason max_rounds) mid-iteration even though the reviewer never asked for an implementation retry. This is the core defect.
  • lib/recovery.mjs mirrors the same round-based check (analyzeLane overLimit/pending/unconfirmed), so lane resume reports the same premature blocked verdict.
  • Operator recovery today is tars lane set-max-rounds --max-rounds <n> --resume — a round-based number that has no relation to the approved plan's iteration structure.

Design overview

Treat handoff round as ordering/correlation metadata only. When the reviewer approves a plan, the verdict carries the review budget the implementation may consume: either an explicit total (review_budget) or a per-iteration allowance (review_budget_per_iteration, total = allowance × iteration_count). TARS persists that budget separately from max_rounds, tracks how many reviewer changes_requested cycles have been dispatched, and blocks only when that budget is exhausted. Iterations, approvals, and implementation responses never consume the budget and never block.

Two distinct limits, explicitly documented. max_rounds becomes the plan-review retry cap: it bounds the pre-approval planning phase of plan-first lanes and remains the fallback cap for direct-build lanes. The new review_budget is the implementation-phase changes-requested budget, chosen by the reviewer at plan approval. They are persisted and enforced independently; see the policies section below.

Implementation is additive and surgical: new verdict fields + validation, two new lane columns with a migration, a shared pure limit helper used by both the coordinator and recovery (so they cannot diverge), a consumption counter incremented at dispatch, a recovery flag on the existing lane set-max-rounds command, docs, and tests.

Explicit policies

1. Plan-review retry policy (pre-approval)

  • While a plan-first lane is in the planning phase (plan not yet approved, planVerdictId unset), plan-phase events — author plan-review, reviewer plan-review-verdict with changes_requested or approved — remain bounded by the lane's max_rounds round cap (default 5). A plan-phase event whose round exceeds max_rounds blocks the lane with reason max_rounds.
  • This cap applies only before approval. Once the approved verdict is processed, max_rounds no longer limits any implementation event; round is pure ordering metadata from then on.
  • Policy is captured in this plan, the review-loop README, and protocols/README.md; the coordinator and recovery enforce it through one shared helper.

2. Implementation review budget (post-approval)

  • An approved plan-review-verdict must carry a positive iteration_count (existing) and exactly the budget: review_budget: <total> (explicit total changes-requested allowance) or review_budget_per_iteration: <n> (per-iteration allowance; total = n × iteration_count). If both are present, the explicit review_budget wins.
  • TARS persists the computed total in the lane row (review_budget) together with a counter (review_budget_consumed, default 0), stored separately from max_rounds.
  • Each implementation-phase code-review handoff with outcome: changes_requested consumes exactly one unit at the moment TARS dispatches it to the author. Iteration transitions, approvals, implementation responses, plan-phase events, and reopen markers never consume the budget.
  • A changes_requested event that would push consumption over the budget blocks the lane with reason review_budget; nothing is dispatched and nothing is consumed. A blocked verdict (plan or code) is the reviewer's explicit stop and is never budget-blocked.
  • A deliberately reopened approved lane (PR feedback) returns to the same rules: its changes_requested cycles consume the same budget; its approvals never do. This keeps the reopen flow bounded and recoverable.

3. Direct-build fallback

  • Direct-build lanes have no plan approval, so no reviewer-selected budget exists. They keep today's behavior: max_rounds caps round for their entire lifecycle. This is the documented fallback policy.
  • A plan-first lane whose verdict somehow lacks a budget (prevented by validation, but defended anyway) falls back to the same round cap for its changes_requested events.

Handoff protocol changes

Approved plan-review verdict (new fields)

---
id: 53-plan-review-1-verdict
type: plan-review-verdict
created_by: reviewer
workflow_id: 53
round: 1
outcome: approved
iteration_count: 3
review_budget_per_iteration: 2   # or review_budget: 6 for the explicit total
responds_to: 53-plan-review-1
---
  • validateWorkflowHandoff (lib/handoff.mjs): an approved plan-review-verdict now requires a positive iteration_count and at least one of review_budget / review_budget_per_iteration (positive integers). Error: approved plan verdict requires positive iteration_count and review_budget or review_budget_per_iteration. tars handoff validate picks this up, so a reviewer who omits the budget is corrected before dispatch.
  • classifyEvent is unchanged (it does not need the new fields; round/outcome/iteration drive routing).
  • The coordinator's plan-review reviewer prompt (lib/coordinator.mjs promptFor) instructs the reviewer to include the budget on approval: include a positive iteration_count, a numbered Implementation Iterations schedule, and the review budget the implementation may consume: either review_budget <total> or review_budget_per_iteration <allowance> (allowance × iteration_count).
  • skills/shared/handoff-review/SKILL.md reviewer section and protocols/README.md document the same fields so every harness teaches its reviewers the contract.

Persistence (lib/state.mjs)

  • Two additive columns on lanes: review_budget INTEGER (NULL = no reviewer-selected budget) and review_budget_consumed INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0. Both are added to CREATE TABLE and to the existing migration ALTER TABLE loop, so pre-existing databases upgrade in place (existing lanes get review_budget NULL → direct-build/fallback behavior).
  • saveLane writes both (lane.reviewBudget ?? null, lane.reviewBudgetConsumed ?? 0); toLane maps them to reviewBudget / reviewBudgetConsumed.

Coordinator (lib/coordinator.mjs)

  • New exported pure helper, shared with recovery: 
    export function laneLimitViolation(lane, event, { alreadyConsumed = false } = {}) {
  if (event.outcome === "blocked") return null
  if (event.reviewKind === "plan" || (lane.planning === "required" && !lane.planVerdictId)) {
    return event.round > lane.maxRounds ? "max_rounds" : null          // planning phase
  }
  if (event.reviewKind !== "code") return null
  if (event.outcome === "changes_requested") {
    if (Number.isInteger(lane.reviewBudget)) {
      return (lane.reviewBudgetConsumed ?? 0) + (alreadyConsumed ? 0 : 1) > lane.reviewBudget
        ? "review_budget" : null
    }
    return event.round > lane.maxRounds ? "max_rounds" : null            // direct-build fallback
  }
  if (!Number.isInteger(lane.reviewBudget)) return event.round > lane.maxRounds ? "max_rounds" : null
  return null                                                           // planned approvals/responses: never capped
}
    The alreadyConsumed flag lets recovery re-check already-dispatched events without double-counting their own consumption (keeps stale-delivery detection working for the last budgeted retry).
  • dispatchLane: replace if (event.round > lane.maxRounds) with const violation = laneLimitViolation(lane, event); block with reason: violation (either "max_rounds" or "review_budget").
  • dispatchLane: when dispatching a code-review changes_requested event to the author (the general author branch), persist reviewBudgetConsumed: (lane.reviewBudgetConsumed ?? 0) + 1 in the same saveLane. No other branch touches the counter.
  • New helper reviewBudgetFor(metadata): explicit review_budget if positive, else review_budget_per_iteration × iterationCountFor(metadata), else null. Both plan-approval paths — the OpenCode compact path and the non-OpenCode direct-build path — persist reviewBudget: reviewBudgetFor(...) on the lane at approval.

Recovery and reporting (lib/recovery.mjs)

  • Import laneLimitViolation; replace every round-based filter:
    • overLimit: !dispatched.has(event.key) && laneLimitViolation(lane, event) → blocked with reason max_rounds or review_budget.
    • pending: !laneLimitViolation(lane, event).
    • unconfirmed: !laneLimitViolation(lane, event, { alreadyConsumed: true }).
  • formatAnalysis prints the budget when set: Review budget: 6 (consumed 2) appended to the State line, so a blocked-at-budget lane is diagnosable at a glance.

CLI (cli.mjs, lib/lane.mjs)

  • Rename setLaneMaxRounds → setLaneLimits({ state, worktreePath, maxRounds, reviewBudget, resume }); updates whichever of maxRounds / reviewBudget is provided (at least one required), keeps the existing opt-in --resume semantics (unblocks blocked → planning or implementing by phase). The consumption counter is never reset by raising the budget.
  • tars lane set-max-rounds gains an optional --review-budget <number> flag; the command requires at least one of --max-rounds / --review-budget, prints both limits, and is the recovery surface for budget exhaustion (--review-budget 10 --resume). The command name is kept for compatibility.
  • Usage text updated to tars lane set-max-rounds --worktree <path> --max-rounds <number> [--review-budget <number>] [--resume].
  • No new budget flag on lane start / lane register / start: the approved verdict is the source of the implementation budget (see out of scope).

Tests

Run: node --test automations/review-loop/test/*.test.mjs (existing suites remain green; 84 tests today).

  • Three-iteration plan (acceptance 1, 3, 6): approved verdict iteration_count: 3 → drive iterations 1–3 with ordinary approvals; the lane reaches approved and reviewBudgetConsumed === 0 even though round exceeds max_rounds; no blocking occurs.
  • Budget carried by the verdict (acceptance 1): explicit review_budget: 6 and review_budget_per_iteration: 2 (with count 3) both persist reviewBudget === 6 on the lane via both approval paths (OpenCode compact and non-OpenCode).
  • Persisted separately (acceptance 2): lane row round-trips reviewBudget / reviewBudgetConsumed through saveLane/lane; legacy rows migrate with reviewBudget null, consumed 0.
  • Repeated changes-requested (acceptance 4): several changes_requested cycles in one iteration each dispatch and increment consumption; approvals interspersed never consume.
  • Exhaustion (acceptance 4): budget 1 → first changes_requested dispatches (consumed 1); second blocks with reason review_budget, sends nothing, state blocked.
  • Plan-review retry policy (acceptance 5): pre-approval plan-review at round > max_rounds still blocks with max_rounds; after approval, a high-round approval/response never blocks. Direct-build lane round cap is unchanged (existing test).
  • Recovery/reporting (acceptance 6): analyzeLane reports blocked (review_budget) for a queued over-budget changes_requested; formatAnalysis prints Review budget: N (consumed M); setLaneLimits({ reviewBudget: 3, resume: true }) then resumeLane --dispatch dispatches exactly once; a dispatched last-budgeted retry with no advancement still reports stale_delivery (not blocked).
  • Validation (acceptance 1): approved verdict without a budget field is invalid; with either form is valid.

Out of scope

  • Pre-setting a review budget via lane start / lane register / start flags; the approved verdict is the authoritative source, and direct-build lanes keep max_rounds.
  • Storing/enforcing the per-iteration allowance granularly; only the computed total is persisted and enforced.
  • Changing direct-build lane semantics or the post-approval reopen handoff format.
  • Resetting review_budget_consumed at approval or on reopen; consumption is monotonic history and the operator raises the budget when recovery is needed.
